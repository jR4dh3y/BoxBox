## Missing or partial, ranked

1. **Session and tab lifecycle** (rows 8–16): no new windows, tab detaching/reordering, close confirmation, or tab/session restore; these are core ways users manage multiple locations and avoid losing work.
2. **OS-aware navigation and Places** (rows 31–37, 48–49, 56): Recent, Desktop/Templates, network browsing, show-and-select integration, and safe navigation before device unmount are central desktop file-manager behaviors.
3. **Navigation and focus parity** (rows 17–18, 27–30, 38–39): missing tab shortcuts, mouse Back/Forward, history menus, middle-click targets, hardware Search, and search-mode details make routine keyboard/mouse browsing inconsistent.
4. **View, sidebar, and preview controls** (rows 20–24, 41–45, 50–55): no hideable tree/shortcuts pane, compact view, zoom, per-folder view settings, persistent image preview pane, or hideable statusbar; these affect frequent browsing and accessibility of dense folders.
5. **Window/menu customization and context** (rows 4–6, 59–69): the fixed title, absent main menus, limited toolbar controls, and lack of toolbar/context-menu customization reduce discoverability and desktop integration.
6. **Safety and recovery** (rows 3, 14, 21, 32, 53, 65): missing root warning, close confirmations, directory-creation prompt, selected-item Trash controls, and Redo increase the cost of mistakes.
7. **Optional integration/extensions** (rows 40, 42, 70): Catfish, metadata highlighting, and custom actions are useful but less fundamental than native navigation, session, and operation recovery.
